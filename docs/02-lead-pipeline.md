# 02 · Lead pipeline

## Stages

```
New → Contacted → Qualified → Booked → Paid consultation → Proposal → Won / Lost
```

Each stage change writes a timestamp. That gives you speed-to-lead, booking rate and paid-conversion rate for free.

## 1. Speed-to-lead (target: under 60 seconds)

The first reply decides most outcomes. On every new row:

1. Send an instant WhatsApp or email acknowledgement in the lead's language.
2. Include one clear next step: a short qualifying question or a booking link.
3. Alert the owner internally (Telegram, Slack or email) with a one-line summary.

WhatsApp note: outside the 24-hour customer-service window you may only send **pre-approved templates**. Get the templates approved before launch, not after.

## 2. Scoring (0–100)

Keep it simple and explainable. Example weights:

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Service the firm actually sells | +25 |
| Timeline within 3 months | +20 |
| Budget or ability to pay confirmed | +20 |
| Complete contact details | +10 |
| Replied to first message | +15 |
| Came from a referral | +10 |

`70+` hot → senior person same day. `40–69` warm → booking link and chase cadence. `<40` nurture → email sequence only.

## 3. Routing

Route by `brand` + `service` + `language` to a named owner. Round-robin only inside a team that sells the same thing.

## 4. Booking and the paid consultation

- Booking link with real calendar availability.
- For paid consultations, take payment at booking. A small fee filters out time-wasters.
- Reminders at 24 hours and 2 hours. No-show? Automatic rebooking message the same day.

## 5. Chase cadence

Day 0, 1, 3, 7, 14, then monthly nurture. Every chase stops the moment the lead replies, books or opts out.

## 6. Reporting

One weekly report per brand: leads by source, speed-to-lead, booked, paid, won, and the single biggest drop-off.
